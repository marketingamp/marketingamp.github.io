Framer Review 2026: The Best Website Builder for Designers?

Updated May 12, 2026 • 15 min read

Framer has exploded in popularity among designers, startups, and creative agencies. It promises pixel-perfect design control with the performance of a modern web framework — no code required. The pitch is compelling: design your website exactly the way you envision it, with professional-grade animations and interactions, and publish it live without touching a line of code.

But does it live up to the hype? We built three complete websites on Framer over four weeks — a SaaS landing page, a portfolio site, and a blog — to test every major feature. Here is our honest assessment.

Design Capabilities: Where Framer Excels

If you have ever felt constrained by the rigid grid systems and limited layout options in Squarespace or Wix, Framer will feel like a revelation. The design canvas works more like Figma than a traditional website builder. You have true freeform layout control, with the ability to place elements exactly where you want them.

Framer uses a component-based design system similar to what professional developers use. You can create reusable components with variants (hover states, active states, different screen sizes), build design systems with shared styles and colors, and maintain consistency across your entire site by editing a component once and having changes propagate everywhere.

The animation and interaction tools are a standout feature. You can create scroll-triggered animations, hover effects, page transitions, and micro-interactions that would normally require JavaScript. The animation editor is visual and intuitive — you define start and end states, set timing curves, and preview in real time. The results are genuinely impressive and rival what a frontend developer would build by hand.

Responsive design is handled through breakpoints, and Framer gives you granular control over how each element behaves at different screen sizes. You can adjust layout, spacing, font sizes, and visibility per breakpoint. The responsive preview updates instantly as you make changes.

CMS: Content Management Built In

Framer's CMS lets you create collections (like blog posts, team members, portfolio projects, or product features) and build dynamic pages that pull from those collections. The CMS editor is clean and straightforward — it supports rich text, images, dates, numbers, booleans, colors, links, and references between collections.

For a blog, you create a Blog Post collection with fields for title, content, featured image, author, and publish date, then design a blog listing page and a blog post template. Framer handles the rest: generating individual pages for each post, populating the listing page, and creating proper URL structures.

The CMS is not as powerful as WordPress or a headless CMS like Sanity, but for most marketing sites and portfolios, it handles content needs well. Where it falls short is complex content relationships, filtering, and search. If your site has hundreds of CMS items or needs advanced querying, you may outgrow Framer's CMS.

SEO Features

Framer takes SEO seriously. Every page gets customizable title tags, meta descriptions, and Open Graph tags. The platform generates clean, semantic HTML and handles technical SEO fundamentals well: proper heading structure, image alt text fields, automatic sitemap generation, and robots.txt management.

Page speed is where Framer's SEO advantage really shows. Because Framer generates static HTML with optimized assets, sites built on Framer consistently score 90+ on Google PageSpeed Insights. In our tests, all three of our Framer sites scored between 92 and 98 on mobile performance — numbers that Squarespace and Wix struggle to match.

The platform also supports custom URL slugs, 301 redirects, canonical URLs, and structured data (via custom code injection). For a no-code builder, the SEO toolkit is impressively complete.

Templates and Starting Points

Framer's template marketplace has grown significantly. There are hundreds of free and premium templates covering SaaS landing pages, portfolios, agency sites, personal blogs, e-commerce storefronts, and more. The quality of the templates is noticeably higher than what you find on most website builders — they look like custom-designed sites rather than cookie-cutter themes.

The Framer community also contributes components, sections, and design elements that you can remix and use in your own projects. This ecosystem of shared resources means you rarely need to design everything from scratch.

If you prefer to start from a blank canvas, Framer's AI site generator can create a starter site from a text description. The AI output is a reasonable starting point that gives you a basic layout, color scheme, and content structure to customize.

Performance

Framer-built sites are fast. The platform generates static HTML and serves it through a global CDN, which means pages load quickly regardless of where your visitors are located. In our testing, time-to-first-byte averaged under 100ms and full page loads completed in under 1.5 seconds on desktop.

Image optimization is automatic. Framer compresses and serves images in modern formats (WebP, AVIF) at appropriate sizes based on the visitor's screen. Lazy loading is built in for images below the fold. These optimizations happen without any configuration — you just upload your images and Framer handles the rest.

The animations and interactions that Framer enables are rendered efficiently. Even pages with complex scroll animations maintained smooth 60fps performance in our tests on mid-range hardware. This is an area where Framer's technical foundation provides a real advantage over builders that bolt on animations as afterthoughts.

The Mini plan at $5/month is excellent for simple portfolio sites or single-page landing pages. For most small business websites, the Basic plan at $15/month hits the sweet spot with enough pages, visitors, and CMS capacity. Compared to Squarespace ($16-49/mo), Webflow ($14-39/mo for site plans), and Wix ($17-159/mo), Framer's pricing is competitive — especially considering the design capabilities you get.

Who Should Use Framer?

Our Verdict

Best for: Designers, creative agencies, startups, and anyone who wants pixel-perfect control over their website without writing code. If you come from a design background and find traditional website builders limiting, Framer is the tool you have been waiting for.

Great for: SaaS landing pages, portfolios, agency websites, personal brands, and marketing sites where design quality and performance matter.

Skip it if: You need robust e-commerce (use Shopify), a heavy-duty CMS (use WordPress or Webflow), or the simplest possible setup with zero learning curve (use Squarespace).

Bottom line: Framer is the best website builder for people who care about design. It gives you creative control that no other no-code tool can match, backed by performance that rivals hand-coded sites. At $15/month for the Basic plan, it is remarkably good value for what you get.
