What Is Leadpages?

Leadpages is a landing page and website builder designed for small businesses, solopreneurs, and marketers who want to create high-converting pages without coding. Founded in 2012, it has grown to serve over 40,000 customers and has helped generate over 8 million leads through its platform.

Beyond landing pages, Leadpages offers alert bars, pop-up forms, a basic website builder, checkout pages for selling digital products, and integrations with every major email marketing platform. Think of it as a conversion toolkit focused on turning traffic into leads and sales.

The Drag-and-Drop Builder

The builder is Leadpages' strongest feature. It uses a section-based layout system where you work with pre-designed page sections (hero areas, feature blocks, testimonial sections, CTA blocks) and customize them by swapping text, images, colors, and fonts.

The learning curve is minimal. In our testing, a team member with no design experience was able to create and publish a professional-looking lead capture page in under 25 minutes, starting from a template. The interface is clean, drag-and-drop is responsive, and the preview system accurately shows how pages will look on desktop, tablet, and mobile.

Design constraints are both a strength and a limitation. Leadpages uses a grid-based layout system that prevents you from creating ugly, misaligned pages — elements snap to a grid and maintain consistent spacing. This is great for beginners because it is nearly impossible to create a bad-looking page. However, experienced designers who want pixel-perfect control will find it restrictive. You cannot freely position elements, layer components, or create custom animations like you can in Unbounce or Framer.

One notable addition in 2026 is the AI copy assistant, which can generate headline variations, body copy, and CTA text based on your industry and target audience. It is helpful for overcoming writer's block but should be treated as a starting point rather than final copy.

Templates: 250+ and Conversion-Focused

Leadpages offers over 250 templates organized by industry (real estate, fitness, consulting, etc.) and purpose (lead capture, webinar registration, sales page, thank you page, etc.). Every template is mobile-responsive out of the box.

The standout feature is that templates are sorted by conversion rate. Leadpages has enough data from its 40,000+ customers to know which page layouts and designs generate the highest opt-in rates. Templates labeled as top-converting are based on aggregate performance data, which gives you a legitimate advantage over starting from scratch.

Template quality is consistently high. The designs look modern and professional, with clean typography, strong visual hierarchy, and clear CTAs. They follow established landing page best practices without requiring you to know what those are.

The template library is smaller than Unbounce's (which offers 100+ with more design variety) but the average quality is higher, and the conversion-rate sorting is a feature unique to Leadpages.

Conversion Tools

Leadpages goes beyond basic landing pages with a suite of conversion tools designed to capture leads across your entire website:

Alert Bars

Sticky notification bars that appear at the top or bottom of any page on your website. You can use them for announcements, limited-time offers, or lead capture with an embedded email field. Alert bars can be triggered by time delay, scroll depth, or exit intent. They are surprisingly effective — in our testing, adding an alert bar to existing blog posts increased opt-in rates by 12-18%.

Pop-Up Forms

Customizable pop-up forms that can be triggered by clicks, time delays, scroll percentage, or exit intent. The pop-up builder uses the same drag-and-drop interface as the landing page builder, so there is no additional learning curve. You can create multi-step pop-ups (a yes/no question followed by an opt-in form) which tend to convert higher than standard single-step forms.

Checkout Pages

Leadpages includes built-in checkout functionality for selling digital products, coaching sessions, and services. You can create payment pages with Stripe integration, apply coupon codes, and sell recurring subscriptions. The checkout pages are basic compared to dedicated ecommerce platforms, but for coaches and consultants selling a course or booking package, they eliminate the need for a separate payment tool.

Leadmeter

The Leadmeter is a built-in scoring system that analyzes your landing page in real time and suggests improvements to increase conversions. It checks your headline, form placement, CTA button design, and page load speed, then gives you an overall conversion score. Think of it as a basic conversion rate optimization consultant built into the editor. The suggestions are practical and actionable, though experienced marketers will find them obvious.

A/B Testing

Split testing is available on the Pro plan ($99/month) and above. You can create variants of any landing page and split traffic between them to test different headlines, images, layouts, CTA copy, or form fields. Leadpages automatically determines statistical significance and shows you which variant wins.

The A/B testing implementation is straightforward but basic. You can test one variable at a time (no multivariate testing), and the reporting shows conversion rates and confidence levels without deeper analytics like heatmaps or session recordings. For most small businesses, this level of testing is sufficient. For advanced optimization, you would pair Leadpages with a dedicated tool like Hotjar or VWO.

It is worth noting that A/B testing is not available on the Standard plan ($49/month), which is a significant limitation. If conversion optimization is important to your business, budget for the Pro plan.

Integrations

Leadpages integrates with over 90 marketing tools, covering the most important categories:

  • Email marketing: Mailchimp, Kit (ConvertKit), ActiveCampaign, AWeber, Drip, MailerLite, HubSpot, and more
  • CRM: HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho, Pipedrive
  • Webinar platforms: GoToWebinar, WebinarJam, Zoom
  • Analytics: Google Analytics, Facebook Pixel, Google Tag Manager
  • Payments: Stripe
  • Automation: Zapier (connects to 5,000+ apps)
  • WordPress: Native plugin for publishing pages directly to your WordPress site

The WordPress plugin is particularly useful. It lets you publish Leadpages landing pages as pages on your WordPress site, maintaining your domain and URL structure. This means you can use your existing WordPress site for content and Leadpages for dedicated conversion pages without visitors seeing a domain switch.

Analytics and Reporting

Every Leadpages page includes built-in analytics tracking page views, unique visitors, conversions, and conversion rates. The dashboard shows performance over time so you can spot trends and seasonal patterns.

The analytics are adequate for understanding basic page performance but limited compared to what Google Analytics provides. We recommend using Leadpages' built-in analytics for quick conversion rate checks and Google Analytics (or your preferred analytics tool) for deeper traffic analysis and attribution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Leadpages good for beginners?

Yes. Leadpages is one of the most beginner-friendly landing page builders available. The drag-and-drop editor requires no coding knowledge, the 250+ templates provide professional starting points, and most users can publish their first landing page within 30 minutes. The learning curve is significantly easier than Unbounce or Instapage.

Does Leadpages include A/B testing?

A/B testing is available on the Pro plan ($99/month, or $74/month billed annually) and above. The Standard plan does not include split testing. On the Pro plan, you can test different headlines, images, CTAs, and entire page layouts to optimize conversion rates.

Can Leadpages replace a full website?

Leadpages offers a basic website builder that can work for simple sites, but it is not a replacement for dedicated website builders like WordPress or Squarespace. It is best used alongside your existing website for dedicated landing pages, lead capture pages, and campaign-specific pages.
