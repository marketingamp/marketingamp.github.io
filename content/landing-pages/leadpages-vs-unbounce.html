Leadpages vs Unbounce: Which Converts Better in 2026?

Updated May 12, 2026 • 14 min read

Leadpages and Unbounce are two of the most popular landing page builders on the market, but they serve different audiences. Leadpages is built for small businesses and solopreneurs who want affordable, easy-to-launch pages. Unbounce is geared toward marketers and agencies that need advanced conversion optimization tools.

We built landing pages on both platforms, ran A/B tests, connected integrations, and pushed each builder to its limits. This is what we found.

Templates

Leadpages offers over 250 templates, all included with every plan. They're organized by industry and conversion goal — lead generation, sales pages, webinar signups, coming-soon pages, and more. The templates are clean and modern, and most are mobile-responsive out of the box. For small business owners who want to pick a template and launch fast, this library is hard to beat.

Unbounce takes a different approach. Its template library is smaller at around 100 designs, but the platform compensates with Smart Builder — an AI-powered tool that generates custom page layouts based on your industry, campaign goal, and audience. Instead of browsing a gallery, you answer a few questions and the AI builds a starting point tailored to your use case. It works surprisingly well for PPC landing pages.

Winner: Leadpages for sheer variety and ease of browsing. Unbounce is better if you want AI-generated layouts customized to your campaign goals.

Drag-and-Drop Builder

This is where the two platforms diverge most sharply.

Leadpages uses a grid-based builder. Elements snap into rows and columns, which keeps pages looking clean even if you have no design experience. The trade-off is less creative freedom — you can't place an element at an arbitrary pixel position. For most landing pages, though, the grid system is an advantage. It prevents you from creating layouts that look broken on mobile.

Unbounce gives you freeform, pixel-level control. You can drag any element anywhere on the canvas. This is powerful for designers and agencies that need precise layouts, but it also means you can easily create pages that don't translate well to smaller screens. Unbounce added a separate mobile editor to address this — you can adjust the mobile layout independently, but it adds an extra step to every build.

Winner: Depends on your skill level. Leadpages is faster and safer for beginners. Unbounce gives advanced users the creative control they need.

A/B Testing

Both platforms include A/B testing, but Unbounce goes significantly further.

Leadpages lets you create page variants and split traffic between them. You can test headlines, images, CTAs, and full page layouts. The reporting is straightforward — you see conversion rates for each variant and can pick a winner. It does the job, and the fact that A/B testing is included on all plans (not locked behind a premium tier) is a genuine advantage.

Unbounce matches all of that and adds Smart Traffic, its standout AI feature. Smart Traffic automatically routes each visitor to the page variant most likely to convert them, based on attributes like location, device, browser, and time of day. Instead of waiting weeks for statistical significance, Smart Traffic starts optimizing after just 50 visits. Unbounce claims it lifts conversions by an average of 30%, and in our testing, we saw a measurable improvement over standard A/B splits within the first few days.

Winner: Unbounce. Smart Traffic is a genuine competitive advantage that no other landing page builder matches.

Integrations

Leadpages connects natively with over 90 tools, including Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign, ConvertKit, HubSpot, Salesforce, Stripe, PayPal, Google Analytics, Facebook Ads, and Zapier. The Zapier integration extends its reach to thousands of additional apps. Setting up integrations is simple — most require just an API key or OAuth connection.

Unbounce offers around 60 native integrations covering similar ground: email marketing platforms, CRMs, analytics tools, and ad networks. It also supports Zapier and direct webhooks for custom setups. The webhook support is particularly useful for agencies that need to push lead data into proprietary systems.

Both platforms integrate with WordPress, letting you publish landing pages directly to your existing site.

Winner: Leadpages by a slim margin, with more native integrations and the same Zapier fallback.

Pricing

This is where Leadpages holds its biggest advantage.

Leadpages pricing (billed annually):

  • Standard: $49/mo — 1 site, unlimited landing pages, pop-ups, alert bars, free hosting, A/B testing
  • Pro: $99/mo — 3 sites, online sales and payments, email trigger links, priority support
  • Advanced: Custom pricing — 50 sites, advanced integrations, dedicated success coach

Unbounce pricing (billed annually):

  • Build: $99/mo — unlimited landing pages, pop-ups, sticky bars, 1 domain
  • Experiment: $149/mo — A/B testing, Smart Traffic, 2 domains
  • Optimize: $249/mo — advanced targeting, audience insights, 3 domains
  • Concierge: $649/mo — dedicated success manager, migration support, 5+ domains

At the entry level, Leadpages is half the price of Unbounce and already includes A/B testing. To get A/B testing on Unbounce, you need the $149/mo Experiment plan. For a small business running one or two campaigns, that price gap is significant.

Winner: Leadpages. It delivers strong core features at a price that's accessible to small businesses and solopreneurs.

Support

Leadpages provides email and chat support on all plans, with phone support and priority response times on Pro and above. Their knowledge base is thorough, with video walkthroughs for most features. They also run regular webinars and offer a small business marketing course library. For users who are new to landing pages, the educational resources are a real bonus.

Unbounce offers email and chat support across all plans, with priority support on higher tiers. Their knowledge base is detailed and well-organized. The Unbounce Community forum and CRO blog provide ongoing optimization advice. For agencies, the Concierge plan includes a dedicated success manager and migration assistance.

Winner: Tie. Both offer solid support. Leadpages edges ahead for beginners with its educational content; Unbounce is better for agencies with dedicated account management on higher plans.

Which Should You Choose?

Our Verdict

Choose Leadpages if: You're a small business owner, solopreneur, or coach who needs to launch landing pages quickly without a big budget. Leadpages gives you everything you need — templates, A/B testing, integrations, and payment processing — at a price that won't break the bank. If you're building your first landing page or running a handful of campaigns, Leadpages is the smarter investment.

Choose Unbounce if: You're a performance marketer, PPC agency, or growth team running high-traffic campaigns where a 10–20% conversion lift translates to real revenue. Smart Traffic alone can justify the higher price if you're spending meaningful money on ads. The freeform builder and advanced targeting make it the right tool when optimization matters more than simplicity.

Bottom line: Leadpages wins on value and ease of use. Unbounce wins on conversion optimization and design flexibility. Neither is objectively better — the right choice depends on your budget, traffic volume, and how much time you want to spend optimizing.
