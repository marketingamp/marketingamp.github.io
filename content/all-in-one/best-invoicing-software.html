Best Invoicing & Accounting Software for Freelancers (2026)

Updated May 12, 2026 • 10 min read

Getting paid shouldn't be the hardest part of freelancing. But if you're still creating invoices in Google Docs, manually tracking expenses in a spreadsheet, and dreading tax season, you're burning hours on admin work that proper invoicing software handles automatically.

The right tool sends professional invoices in under a minute, tracks who's paid and who hasn't, categorizes expenses, and generates the reports your accountant needs at tax time. Some even handle payment processing so clients can pay by credit card or bank transfer directly from the invoice.

We tested five invoicing and accounting tools specifically for freelancers and solopreneurs — people billing clients, not managing payroll for 50 employees. Here's what we found.

1. FreshBooks — Best for Client-Facing Freelancers

FreshBooks was built for freelancers, and it shows in every interaction. Creating an invoice takes about 45 seconds: select a client, add line items, and hit send. The invoice arrives branded with your logo and colors, and includes a "Pay Now" button so clients can pay by credit card, bank transfer, or Apple Pay without leaving the invoice.

What makes FreshBooks stand out is its client experience. Automated payment reminders chase overdue invoices for you (tactfully — we checked). Late payment fees can be added automatically. You can see exactly when a client opens your invoice, which is useful for knowing whether to follow up or wait. In our test, clients consistently commented that FreshBooks invoices looked more professional than competitors.

Time tracking is built in, which matters for freelancers who bill hourly. Start a timer, assign it to a project, and those hours automatically populate your next invoice. The expense tracking connects to your bank account and categorizes transactions, though the auto-categorization needs manual corrections about 15% of the time.

The Lite plan at $19/month supports up to 5 billable clients, which covers most solo freelancers. The Plus plan at $33/month removes the client limit and adds proposals, recurring expenses, and double-entry accounting. For freelancers earning over $3,000/month, the time saved on invoicing and expense tracking pays for the subscription many times over.

Pros

  • Fastest invoicing workflow — under 60 seconds per invoice
  • Automated payment reminders reduce late payments
  • Built-in time tracking that flows into invoices
  • Client portal shows invoice history and outstanding balances
  • Professional-looking invoices with online payment options

Cons

  • Lite plan limited to 5 billable clients
  • Not as robust for full accounting as QuickBooks
  • Payment processing fees (2.9% + $0.30 per transaction)

2. QuickBooks Self-Employed — Best for Full Accounting + Invoicing

If you need more than invoicing — think mileage tracking, quarterly tax estimates, Schedule C categorization — QuickBooks Self-Employed is the industry standard for good reason. It does everything FreshBooks does on the invoicing side, plus it handles the accounting complexity that freelancers face at tax time.

The killer feature is automatic tax categorization. Connect your bank account, and QuickBooks separates business from personal expenses, categorizes them by Schedule C line items, and estimates your quarterly tax payments in real time. During our test, the estimated quarterly payment it calculated was within $50 of what our accountant independently computed.

QuickBooks Simple Start at $30/month is the plan most freelancers need. It includes invoicing, expense tracking, receipt capture (snap a photo and it extracts the data), mileage tracking, and basic reporting. The Self-Employed plan at $15/month is cheaper but lacks features like bill management and bank reconciliation.

The interface is more complex than FreshBooks — it's accounting software that happens to do invoicing, rather than invoicing software that happens to do accounting. First-time users should budget an hour for initial setup.

Pros

  • Most comprehensive accounting for freelancers
  • Automatic quarterly tax estimates
  • Schedule C expense categorization
  • Receipt scanning via mobile app
  • Accountant can access your books directly

Cons

  • Steeper learning curve than FreshBooks
  • Interface can feel cluttered for simple invoicing needs
  • Pricing has increased significantly in recent years

3. Wave — Best Free Invoicing & Accounting

Wave is genuinely free for invoicing and accounting — not a crippled trial, not a freemium hook. You get unlimited invoicing, unlimited clients, expense tracking, financial reporting, and receipt scanning at no cost. Wave makes money from optional payment processing (2.9% + $0.60 per credit card transaction) and payroll services.

For freelancers who don't need time tracking or automated payment reminders, Wave provides 90% of what FreshBooks offers at 100% less cost. The invoice templates are clean and professional, clients can pay online, and the accounting reports (profit & loss, balance sheet, tax summaries) are solid enough for most accountants.

The limitations are real but manageable. There's no built-in time tracking, no project management, no mobile receipt scanning in the free plan (it's a paid add-on), and customer support is limited to self-serve resources on the free plan. If you're bootstrapping and every dollar matters, Wave is the clear choice until your business outgrows it.

  • Online payment acceptance (credit card and bank transfer)

  • Higher payment processing fees than competitors
  • Limited customer support on free plan

4. Xero — Best for Growing Freelance Businesses

Xero is the strongest choice for freelancers who are transitioning from solo work to running a small business with contractors, recurring revenue, or multiple income streams. It handles bank reconciliation, multi-currency invoicing, inventory tracking, and purchase orders — features you won't need today but might need in six months.

The Starter plan at $15/month allows 20 invoices and 5 bills per month. The Standard plan at $42/month removes those limits and adds multi-currency support. Xero's app marketplace has over 1,000 integrations, making it the most extensible option on this list.

Xero is popular with accountants internationally (especially in the UK, Australia, and New Zealand), so if you work with an accountant who already uses Xero, sticking with it avoids compatibility headaches.

5. Zoho Invoice — Best for International Freelancers

Zoho Invoice stands out for freelancers working with international clients. It supports invoicing in multiple currencies and languages, handles tax compliance for various jurisdictions, and integrates tightly with the broader Zoho ecosystem (Zoho CRM, Zoho Books, Zoho Projects).

The free plan supports up to 5 clients with unlimited invoices, which is enough for freelancers with a focused client base. The Standard plan at $15/month removes the client limit and adds recurring invoices, project billing, and advanced reporting.

The interface is clean and functional, though it lacks the personality of FreshBooks. If you're already using other Zoho products, the seamless integration makes Zoho Invoice the obvious choice.

Which Invoicing Software Should You Choose?

Our Verdict

For most freelancers: FreshBooks is the best overall choice. Its invoicing workflow is the fastest, automated payment reminders reduce late payments, and built-in time tracking eliminates a separate tool. The Lite plan at $19/month is a bargain for the time it saves.

For tax-conscious freelancers: QuickBooks Self-Employed takes the headache out of tax season with automatic Schedule C categorization and quarterly estimates. If tax time stresses you out, QuickBooks is worth the premium.

For bootstrappers on zero budget: Wave gives you professional invoicing and real accounting software at no cost. Start here and upgrade to FreshBooks or QuickBooks when your revenue justifies it.

For growing businesses: Xero scales the best, with features like multi-currency, inventory, and 1,000+ integrations that you'll appreciate as your freelance practice grows into a business.
