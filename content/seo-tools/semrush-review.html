Semrush Review 2026: Is It Still the Best SEO Tool?

Updated May 12, 2026 • 18 min read

Semrush has been a dominant force in the SEO tool market for over a decade. It started as a keyword research tool and has grown into a full-scale digital marketing platform used by more than 10 million marketers worldwide. But in a market where competitors keep shipping new features, does Semrush still deserve the top spot?

We've been using Semrush across multiple client projects and our own sites for the past three years. This review is based on daily, hands-on use — not a weekend trial. We'll cover every major feature, break down whether the pricing makes sense at each tier, and tell you exactly who should buy it and who should look elsewhere.

What Is Semrush?

Semrush is an all-in-one SEO and digital marketing platform. At its core, it helps you find keywords, audit your website for technical issues, analyze your competitors' strategies, track your search rankings, and build a content strategy grounded in real data.

But calling Semrush "just" an SEO tool undersells it. Over the years it has expanded into PPC research, social media management, content marketing, PR monitoring, and local SEO. Whether that breadth is a strength or a distraction depends entirely on what you need — and we'll get into that below.

Key Features

1. Keyword Research

Keyword research is the foundation of Semrush, and it remains the strongest part of the platform. The Keyword Magic Tool lets you enter a seed keyword and instantly generates thousands of related terms, organized into topic clusters. Each keyword comes with search volume, keyword difficulty, CPC, competitive density, SERP features, and trend data.

What makes this genuinely useful (not just a wall of data) is the filtering. You can narrow results by difficulty, volume range, word count, SERP features, and intent type — informational, navigational, commercial, or transactional. That intent filter alone saves significant research time. Instead of manually sorting through keywords, you can immediately isolate terms where people are ready to buy versus terms where they're just browsing.

The Keyword Gap tool is equally valuable for competitive work. Enter your domain alongside up to four competitors, and Semrush shows you every keyword they rank for that you don't. This is one of the fastest ways to find content opportunities you're missing.

2. Site Audit

Semrush's Site Audit tool crawls your entire website and flags technical SEO issues: broken links, slow pages, missing meta tags, duplicate content, crawlability problems, HTTPS errors, internal linking issues, and more. It checks over 140 distinct factors and assigns a site health score.

The real value here is prioritization. Every issue is categorized as an error, warning, or notice, so you know what to fix first. The tool also explains each issue in plain language and tells you how to fix it — which matters if you're not a technical SEO specialist.

You can schedule automatic crawls (weekly or monthly) and get notified when new issues appear. For agencies managing multiple sites, this alone justifies a significant chunk of the subscription cost.

3. Backlink Analysis

Semrush's backlink database is massive — over 43 trillion links. The Backlink Analytics tool lets you analyze any domain's link profile: referring domains, anchor text distribution, follow vs. nofollow ratio, link growth over time, and toxic backlinks that could be hurting your rankings.

The Backlink Gap tool works like the Keyword Gap tool but for links. It shows you sites that link to your competitors but not to you, which gives you a targeted list of outreach prospects. We've found this to be one of the highest-ROI features in the entire platform — a focused hour with Backlink Gap regularly surfaces 20–30 realistic link prospects.

The Link Building Tool goes further by letting you manage your outreach campaigns directly inside Semrush. You can find prospects, track your outreach, and monitor acquired links in one workflow. It's not as polished as dedicated outreach tools like Pitchbox, but having it built in reduces tool-switching.

4. Position Tracking

Position Tracking monitors your daily search rankings for any set of target keywords. You can track by device (desktop vs. mobile), location (down to the city level), and language. It also tracks your competitors' positions for the same keywords, so you can see who's gaining and losing ground.

The feature we use most here is the SERP feature tracking. It shows you when your pages appear in featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes, local packs, and other SERP features — and when you lose them. Given that featured snippets can drive significant click-through rates, knowing exactly when you win or lose one is immediately actionable.

Reporting is clean. You can set up automated email reports for clients or stakeholders with exactly the data they need, on a schedule you choose.

5. Content Marketing Toolkit

Semrush's content tools have improved substantially over the past two years. The SEO Content Template analyzes the top 10 results for your target keyword and gives you concrete recommendations: target word count, readability score, semantically related keywords to include, and suggested sources for backlinks.

The SEO Writing Assistant (available as a Google Docs add-on or WordPress plugin) scores your content in real time against these recommendations as you write. It checks readability, SEO optimization, originality, and tone of voice. This is genuinely useful for content teams — it turns abstract SEO guidance into a live checklist.

The Topic Research tool helps with ideation. Enter a broad topic and it generates subtopics, headline ideas, and questions your audience is asking. It's a solid starting point for editorial planning, though it works best as a supplement to your own expertise rather than a replacement for it.

6. Competitive Analysis

This is where Semrush's breadth becomes a clear advantage. You can analyze any competitor's entire digital strategy in one place: their organic keywords, paid keywords, backlink profile, top-performing content, traffic estimates, and advertising history.

The Traffic Analytics tool provides estimated monthly visits, bounce rates, visit duration, and traffic sources for any domain. The data is directional rather than exact (no third-party tool can match your own analytics), but it's reliable enough for competitive benchmarking and spotting trends.

Market Explorer gives you a bird's-eye view of your entire competitive landscape: market size estimates, audience overlap between competitors, traffic trends across the market, and growth benchmarks. For agencies pitching new clients or businesses entering a new market, this kind of overview is invaluable.

Who Is Semrush Best For?

SEO professionals and consultants who need a single tool that covers keyword research, technical audits, backlink analysis, and rank tracking. Semrush eliminates the need to cobble together three or four separate tools.

Digital marketing agencies managing multiple clients. The project management structure, white-label reports, and client-friendly dashboards are built for this use case. The Business plan's API access also supports custom integrations and automated reporting.

Content marketing teams who want data-driven editorial planning. The Guru plan's Content Marketing Toolkit ties keyword research directly into content creation, which keeps SEO and content teams aligned.

Competitive intelligence work. If a significant part of your strategy involves understanding what competitors are doing — their keywords, their backlinks, their ad spend, their content — Semrush's breadth is unmatched.

Who Should Skip It

Bloggers or small site owners on a tight budget. At $139.95/month, Semrush is hard to justify if you're managing a single blog that makes less than a few hundred dollars per month. Tools like Mangools offer core keyword research at a fraction of the cost.

Teams that only need backlink analysis. If your primary use case is link building and backlink research, Ahrefs is arguably stronger in this specific area and costs less at the entry level.

People who want simplicity. Semrush has dozens of tools across its platform. If you want a clean, focused experience without the learning curve, a simpler tool like Mangools or Ubersuggest will feel better.

Semrush vs. the Competition

Semrush vs. Ahrefs

This is the comparison everyone asks about. Ahrefs is Semrush's closest competitor, and both are excellent. The key differences: Semrush has a larger keyword database and stronger competitive analysis tools (especially Traffic Analytics and Market Explorer). Ahrefs has a faster backlink index that updates more frequently and a cleaner, more focused interface. Semrush includes PPC research, social tools, and content marketing features that Ahrefs doesn't. If you need an all-in-one platform, Semrush wins. If you prioritize backlink analysis and want a simpler tool, Ahrefs is the better pick.

Semrush vs. Moz Pro

Moz Pro is a solid SEO tool with a loyal following, but it's fallen behind both Semrush and Ahrefs in terms of data size, feature breadth, and update frequency. Moz's Domain Authority metric remains widely used and its local SEO tools are strong, but its keyword database is significantly smaller than Semrush's. For most users, Semrush offers substantially more value at a comparable price point.

Semrush vs. Mangools

Mangools (KWFinder, SERPChecker, LinkMiner) is the budget-friendly alternative. Starting at $29.90/month, it costs a fraction of Semrush and provides genuinely useful keyword research for small sites. The data is limited compared to Semrush — fewer keywords, smaller backlink index, no site audit — but if your needs are simple and your budget is tight, Mangools covers the basics well. Think of it as the Honda Civic to Semrush's full-size truck: smaller, cheaper, and perfectly fine for lighter loads.

Final Verdict
