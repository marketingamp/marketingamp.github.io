7 Best Semrush Alternatives (Cheaper & Just as Powerful) 2026

Updated May 16, 2026 • 16 min read

Semrush is the Swiss Army knife of SEO — keyword research, site audits, rank tracking, backlink analysis, content planning, and PPC intelligence all under one roof. But at $139.95/month for the Pro plan (and $249.95/month for Guru), it's also one of the most expensive tools in any marketer's stack. That's $1,679 per year before you've published a single optimized page.

The reality is that most small businesses and solo marketers use maybe 30% of Semrush's features. If you primarily need keyword research and rank tracking, you're paying enterprise prices for a fraction of the functionality. Fortunately, the SEO tool market has exploded with capable alternatives — several of which cost 70–80% less.

We put seven Semrush alternatives through the same workflow: keyword research for a niche site, competitor gap analysis, rank tracking over 30 days, and a full technical site audit. Here's how they stacked up.

1. Ahrefs — Best for Backlink Research & Link Building

Ahrefs is Semrush's most direct competitor and the only tool on this list that matches it in raw data depth. Its backlink index is the largest in the industry — over 35 trillion known links — and its crawler updates this data more frequently than any other tool. If link building is a core part of your SEO strategy, Ahrefs is the better choice.

The Content Explorer feature is something Semrush can't fully match. It lets you search a database of billions of web pages by topic, then filter by organic traffic, referring domains, word count, and publication date. It's the fastest way to find link-worthy content in your niche and identify outreach targets.

Where Ahrefs falls short compared to Semrush: it has no PPC research tools, no social media tracking, and its site audit feature, while competent, isn't as granular. But if you care primarily about organic SEO, Ahrefs delivers more backlink data per dollar.

Pros

  • Largest backlink index in the industry (35T+ links)
  • Content Explorer is unmatched for content research
  • Keyword difficulty scores are highly accurate
  • Free Webmaster Tools for basic site analysis

Cons

  • $99/mo starting price is still premium
  • No PPC or advertising research
  • Learning curve for advanced features

2. Mangools — Best Budget SEO Suite

Mangools is the tool we recommend most often to small business owners and solo bloggers who need real SEO data without the enterprise price tag. At $29/month (paid annually), it costs less than a quarter of Semrush while covering the four pillars most people actually need: keyword research, rank tracking, backlink analysis, and SERP analysis.

KWFinder, its keyword research tool, is genuinely one of the best in the business. The interface is cleaner than Semrush's, the keyword difficulty score is remarkably accurate for its price tier, and the search volume data is reliable. For finding low-competition long-tail keywords, KWFinder often surfaces opportunities that Semrush's keyword tool buries in noise.

SERPWatcher handles rank tracking with daily updates, SERPChecker analyzes search results for any keyword, and LinkMiner covers backlink research. None of these match Semrush feature-for-feature, but they cover 80% of what most users need at 20% of the cost.

3. SE Ranking — Best for Rank Tracking

SE Ranking offers the most flexible rank tracking of any tool on this list. You choose exactly how many keywords you want to track, and pricing scales accordingly. Track 250 keywords across multiple search engines with daily updates starting at $44/month — that's roughly what Semrush charges for 500 keywords at $139/month, but SE Ranking includes a full SEO toolkit alongside the tracker.

Beyond rank tracking, SE Ranking packs in a competent keyword research tool, competitive analysis, site audit, backlink checker, and even a white-label reporting feature for agencies. The on-page SEO checker gives you actionable recommendations for individual pages — similar to Semrush's On-Page SEO Checker but at a third of the price.

The platform has grown significantly in the past two years. Its keyword database now covers 190+ countries, and the data quality has caught up to mid-tier competitors. For agencies managing multiple client sites, the white-label and reporting features make SE Ranking particularly attractive.

4. Ubersuggest — Best for SEO Beginners

Neil Patel's Ubersuggest is the most accessible SEO tool on this list. The free tier gives you 3 keyword searches per day with limited data, enough to dip your toes in. The paid plan at $12/month (or a $120 lifetime deal for a single site) makes it the cheapest full-featured option available.

Ubersuggest covers keyword research, content ideas, site audit, backlink data, and rank tracking. The keyword research interface is straightforward — type a keyword, see volume, difficulty, CPC, and related ideas. The content ideas feature shows you the top-ranking pages for any keyword along with their estimated traffic and social shares, which is useful for content planning.

The trade-off is data depth. Ubersuggest's keyword database and backlink index are noticeably smaller than Semrush, Ahrefs, or even Mangools. For competitive niches, you'll miss opportunities that bigger tools would surface. But for local businesses, new blogs, and small niche sites, Ubersuggest provides plenty of data at an unbeatable price.

5. Moz Pro — Best for Domain Authority Research

Moz invented the Domain Authority metric that the entire SEO industry uses as a shorthand for site strength. If you work with clients or stakeholders who speak in DA terms, Moz Pro's data is the authoritative source. No other tool can give you the "official" DA score.

Beyond DA, Moz Pro offers solid keyword research, rank tracking, site crawling, and on-page optimization tools. The Keyword Explorer surfaces "Priority" scores that combine volume, difficulty, and organic CTR into a single actionable number — a useful feature for prioritizing which keywords to target first.

At $49/month for the Starter plan, Moz Pro is reasonably priced. The 30-day free trial is the longest of any tool on this list, giving you plenty of time to evaluate. The main weakness is that Moz's link index is significantly smaller than Ahrefs' or Semrush's, so backlink research is where it falls shortest.

Pros

  • Official source for Domain Authority scores
  • Priority scores simplify keyword prioritization
  • 30-day free trial (longest available)
  • Strong community and educational resources

Cons

6. Surfer SEO — Best for Content Optimization

Surfer SEO takes a different approach than Semrush. Instead of being a general-purpose SEO toolkit, it focuses specifically on content optimization — helping you write pages that rank. Its Content Editor analyzes the top-ranking pages for your target keyword and generates real-time guidelines for word count, headings, keyword density, images, and NLP terms.

This is where Surfer excels beyond anything Semrush offers. While Semrush has a Content Template feature, Surfer's real-time scoring, NLP analysis, and competitor comparison are significantly more detailed. Writers can see their "Content Score" update live as they write, with clear indicators of what to add or remove.

At $89/month, Surfer isn't cheap, but it's a fundamentally different tool than Semrush. Many SEO professionals use Surfer alongside a keyword research tool like Mangools or Ubersuggest, spending $118–$101/month total for keyword research plus content optimization — still less than Semrush alone.

7. Keywords Everywhere — Best for Quick Research

Keywords Everywhere isn't trying to replace Semrush. It's a browser extension that overlays search volume, CPC, and competition data directly on Google, YouTube, Amazon, Bing, and other search engines as you browse. At $2.25/month for 100,000 credits, it's essentially a rounding error in your marketing budget.

The real value is in its workflow integration. Instead of switching to a separate tool every time you want to check a keyword, you see the data right where you're already searching. Related keywords, "People Also Ask" data, and trend charts appear in the sidebar. For content creators who do quick keyword checks dozens of times per day, this saves significant time.

Keywords Everywhere also recently added a competitor gap analysis feature and a keyword difficulty score, bringing it closer to a lightweight Semrush alternative. It won't replace a full SEO suite for serious campaigns, but paired with Google Search Console (free), it covers basic keyword research needs at 98% less cost.

Pros

  • $2.25/mo — practically free
  • Data appears right in Google/YouTube as you search
  • 100,000 credits per month is plenty for most users
  • No context-switching to a separate app

Cons

  • Browser extension only — no standalone dashboard
  • No site audit, rank tracking, or backlink analysis
  • Data is less comprehensive than full SEO suites

Which Semrush Alternative Should You Choose?

Our Verdict

Best overall alternative: Ahrefs matches Semrush in data depth and beats it on backlink research. At $99/mo, it's $41/mo cheaper while being the only tool that truly competes on features.

Best value for money: Mangools at $29/mo covers 80% of what most people need from Semrush. KWFinder alone is worth the price for keyword research.

Best for tight budgets: Ubersuggest at $12/mo (or $120 lifetime) gives you keyword research, site audits, and rank tracking for less than the cost of a lunch.

Best for content creators: Surfer SEO at $89/mo focuses on what matters most — writing content that ranks. Pair it with a cheap keyword tool and you're set.

Best for beginners: Start with Keywords Everywhere ($2.25/mo) plus Google Search Console (free). Upgrade to Mangools when you need more data.

How We Tested

We ran each tool through an identical workflow: researched 50 keywords in the home improvement niche, ran a competitor gap analysis against three sites, tracked 100 keyword rankings over 30 days, and performed a full technical audit on a 200-page site. We evaluated data accuracy by cross-referencing keyword volumes with Google Keyword Planner and checking backlink counts against manual verification.

Pricing was compared based on plans that include at least 500 keyword lookups per day and 500 tracked keywords. Free trials were tested fully, including export capabilities and API access where available.
