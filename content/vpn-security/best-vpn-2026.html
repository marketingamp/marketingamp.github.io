5 Best VPNs in 2026: Speed, Privacy & Value Compared

Updated May 12, 2026 • 14 min read

Picking a VPN used to be simple: you just needed something to hide your IP address. In 2026, the stakes are higher. ISPs sell browsing data by default in most countries, public Wi-Fi attacks are increasingly automated, and geo-restrictions on streaming content have gotten more aggressive than ever.

A good VPN now needs to do three things well: keep your connection fast enough that you forget it's running, protect your privacy with zero compromises, and actually work with the streaming services you pay for. Most VPNs fail at least one of those.

We tested each VPN on this list over four weeks across servers in the US, UK, Germany, Japan, and Australia. We measured real-world download speeds, ran DNS and WebRTC leak tests, checked streaming compatibility with Netflix, Disney+, BBC iPlayer, and Prime Video, and evaluated each provider's privacy policy and audit history. Here's what we found.

1. NordVPN — Best Overall VPN

NordVPN consistently outperforms the competition across the board. It's not the absolute fastest (ExpressVPN edges it out) and it's not the cheapest (Surfshark wins there), but no other VPN balances speed, security, privacy, and usability as well as NordVPN does.

The NordLynx protocol, built on WireGuard, delivers speeds that are nearly indistinguishable from an unprotected connection on most servers. In our tests, we retained 92% of our base speed on nearby servers and 78% on cross-continent connections to Tokyo — more than enough for 4K streaming and large downloads.

Security features go well beyond the basics. Threat Protection Pro blocks ads, trackers, and malicious websites at the DNS level, even when you're not connected to a VPN server. Double VPN routes your traffic through two servers for an extra encryption layer. And Meshnet lets you create your own private network between devices, which is genuinely useful for remote access and LAN gaming.

NordVPN has completed four independent security audits by PricewaterhouseCoopers and Deloitte, confirming its no-logs policy. Its Panama jurisdiction means it has no legal obligation to retain user data.

2. Surfshark — Best Value (Unlimited Devices)

Surfshark's killer feature is something no other major VPN offers: unlimited simultaneous connections on a single account. If you have a household full of phones, laptops, tablets, smart TVs, and a router, you can cover every device without worrying about hitting a limit. At $2.19/month on the 2-year plan, the per-device cost is essentially nothing.

But cheap doesn't mean cheap quality. Surfshark's speeds were solid in our tests — 88% speed retention on nearby servers using WireGuard. It reliably unblocked Netflix in five regions, Disney+, and BBC iPlayer during our testing period. The CleanWeb feature blocks ads and trackers, and the MultiHop option routes through two countries for extra privacy.

Surfshark also includes features that typically cost extra elsewhere. Its Alternative ID tool generates a new online identity with a disposable email for signing up to services. The Surfshark Alert feature monitors data breaches for your email addresses. These are bundled into the Surfshark One plan at no additional cost on higher tiers.

The main trade-off is speed consistency. While average speeds were good, we noticed more variability between servers compared to NordVPN and ExpressVPN. Some servers in congested regions dropped below 70% speed retention during peak hours.

3. ExpressVPN — Fastest Speeds

If raw speed is your top priority, ExpressVPN is the one to beat. Its proprietary Lightway protocol consistently delivered the highest speeds in our testing — 95% speed retention on nearby servers and 83% on transatlantic connections. For users on gigabit connections who don't want their VPN to be a bottleneck, ExpressVPN is the clear choice.

Speed isn't the only thing ExpressVPN does well. Its apps are the most polished and intuitive on this list. The one-tap connect experience works identically across Windows, Mac, iOS, Android, Linux, and router firmware. If you've ever been frustrated by a VPN app that feels like it was designed by engineers for engineers, ExpressVPN is the antidote.

The TrustedServer technology runs all servers on RAM only — no hard drives. Every server is wiped clean on every reboot, making it physically impossible for data to persist. This has been verified by multiple independent audits from Cure53 and KPMG.

The downside is price. ExpressVPN is the most expensive option on this list, and it only allows 8 simultaneous connections (up from 5 in previous years, but still behind NordVPN's 10 and Surfshark's unlimited). For the performance you get, many users will find it worth it — but budget-conscious buyers should look at Surfshark or CyberGhost first.

4. ProtonVPN — Best for Privacy

ProtonVPN is built by the team behind ProtonMail, and privacy is in its DNA. If your primary reason for using a VPN is protecting your personal data — not just streaming or saving a few dollars — ProtonVPN is the strongest choice on this list.

It's headquartered in Switzerland, which has some of the strongest privacy laws in the world and sits outside the Five Eyes, Nine Eyes, and Fourteen Eyes surveillance alliances. ProtonVPN's no-logs policy has been verified by independent audits from Securitum, and every app is open source, meaning anyone can inspect the code for backdoors or vulnerabilities.

The Secure Core feature routes your traffic through privacy-friendly countries (Switzerland, Iceland, Sweden) before sending it to the exit server. Even if an exit server is compromised or monitored, your real IP remains hidden behind the Secure Core layer. This is a meaningful upgrade over standard double-VPN implementations because the Secure Core servers are located in hardened data centers with strict physical access controls.

ProtonVPN is also the only premium VPN that offers a genuinely usable free plan — no data caps, no ads, just limited to servers in five countries with slower speeds. It's an excellent way to test the service before committing.

The trade-off is speed. ProtonVPN's WireGuard implementation is solid but not as fast as NordLynx or Lightway. We measured 85% speed retention on nearby servers, which is good but noticeably behind the top three. Secure Core connections drop that further to around 65%, which is the cost of that extra privacy layer.

5. CyberGhost — Best for Streaming

CyberGhost takes a different approach to VPN servers: it labels them by what they're optimized for. Instead of guessing which server works with Netflix US or BBC iPlayer, you just pick the dedicated streaming server for that service. This isn't just marketing — these servers are actively maintained and rotated to stay ahead of VPN detection.

In our streaming tests, CyberGhost had the highest success rate of any VPN we tested. It unblocked Netflix in 8 regions, Disney+, HBO Max, BBC iPlayer, Amazon Prime Video, and even more restrictive services like DAZN and Hulu. The dedicated streaming servers connected on the first try every time during our four-week test period.

CyberGhost also has the largest server network on this list — over 11,500 servers across 100 countries. That sheer volume means you're less likely to hit a congested server, and you have more options for connecting to specific locations.

At $2.03/month on the 3-year plan, it's the cheapest option here. The catch is that CyberGhost's speeds, while perfectly adequate for streaming and browsing, don't match NordVPN or ExpressVPN for demanding use cases like large file transfers or competitive gaming. We measured 86% speed retention — good, but not great.

Privacy is adequate but not at ProtonVPN's level. CyberGhost is based in Romania (outside the Fourteen Eyes), publishes quarterly transparency reports, and has completed a no-logs audit by Deloitte. But its apps are not open source, and it doesn't offer anything equivalent to Secure Core.

Which VPN Should You Choose?

Our Verdict

For most people: NordVPN is the best all-around choice. It's fast, secure, independently audited, and packed with features like Threat Protection and Meshnet that go beyond basic VPN functionality. It strikes the ideal balance between performance and price.

For families and multi-device households: Surfshark's unlimited device connections and low price make it the obvious pick. One subscription covers every device in your home.

For speed-sensitive users: ExpressVPN is worth the premium if you're on a fast internet connection and don't want to sacrifice any bandwidth. Its Lightway protocol is the fastest we've tested.

For privacy-first users: ProtonVPN is the gold standard. Swiss jurisdiction, open-source code, Secure Core servers, and a free plan that doesn't compromise on principles. If you distrust the VPN industry in general, Proton has done the most to earn that trust back.

For streaming enthusiasts: CyberGhost's dedicated streaming servers make it the most reliable option for accessing geo-restricted content. Its huge server network and low price are a bonus.

How We Tested

We tested each VPN over four weeks using a 500 Mbps base connection. Speed tests were conducted three times daily across five server locations (US East, UK, Germany, Japan, Australia) using Ookla Speedtest and fast.com. We measured download speed, upload speed, and latency for each session.

For privacy, we ran DNS leak tests, WebRTC leak tests, and IPv6 leak tests on each VPN using browserleaks.com and ipleak.net. We reviewed each provider's privacy policy, jurisdiction, audit history, and any past security incidents.

For streaming, we tested each VPN against Netflix (US, UK, Japan, Germany, Australia), Disney+, BBC iPlayer, Amazon Prime Video, HBO Max, and Hulu. A "pass" meant the VPN connected and streamed content without errors. We tested each service weekly to check consistency.
